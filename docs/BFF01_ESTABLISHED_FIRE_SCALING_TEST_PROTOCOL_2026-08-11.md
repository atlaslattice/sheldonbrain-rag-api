# BFF-01 Established-Fire Scaling Test Protocol
## Concert-Scale Vibroacoustic Suppression
**11 August 2026**

**Status:** HYPOTHESIS / PROTOTYPE CANDIDATE. Controlled fire-science testing only; not a recommendation to deploy unvalidated high-SPL systems against uncontrolled wildfires.

## Purpose
Determine whether high-output, large-aperture, phase-controlled professional audio systems can materially alter the behavior or suppressibility of an already-established vegetation fire under controlled conditions.

The central variable is not source dB rating. It is the **actual acoustic field delivered at the combustion zone**: sound pressure, particle velocity, phase structure, induced flow, distance, wind, and coupling to mist/retardant.

## Primary objective
**Maximize extinction probability or measurable weakening of an established vegetation fire, subject to strict safety constraints.**

## Secondary outcomes
Measure changes in extinction probability, time to extinction, flame height/geometry, rate of spread, thermal output/heat-release proxy, ember generation and transport, mist/retardant penetration, reignition probability, acoustic field at target, rear-zone exposure, suppressant consumption, electrical energy use, and non-target effects.

## Candidate test article
- Concert-scale deep-sub array with independent DSP, delay, phase, and level control.
- Cardioid/end-fire or other phase-controlled configuration for forward reinforcement and rear rejection.
- Synchronized mist or ecologically acceptable suppression medium.
- Thermal IR cameras.
- LiDAR/ranging where useful.
- Distributed microphones / low-frequency pressure sensors.
- Particle-velocity instrumentation where feasible.
- Weather stations / anemometers.
- Calibrated flow and droplet characterization.
- Independent conventional suppression capable of terminating every test regardless of BFF performance.

**144 dB-class** is a source-capability envelope, not a magic target threshold. Record the actual field at the flame.

## Fire environment
Use a sanctioned fire-science facility, burn pad/chamber, or professionally managed prescribed-burn environment with legal authority, experienced fire personnel, predetermined containment, and conventional suppression already staged.

Document fuel species, geometry, moisture, mass loading, ignition energy, slope, wind, temperature, humidity, and atmospheric stability.

## Core experimental arms
1. Established fire, no experimental suppression.
2. Mist/retardant only.
3. Acoustics only.
4. **BFF:** synchronized acoustics + mist/retardant.
5. Optional directional/cardioid BFF vs acoustically equivalent non-directional configuration.
6. Optional closed-loop targeting vs fixed targeting.

Randomize trial order where safe and practical. Replicate conditions sufficiently to estimate uncertainty.

## Acoustic envelope
Sweep frequency rather than preselecting one special value. For each run record:
- source configuration;
- amplifier power;
- frequency/waveform/harmonics;
- phase/delay solution;
- source-to-fire distance;
- SPL at multiple fire-front locations;
- particle velocity where feasible;
- rear-zone SPL;
- wind/turbulence;
- ground/terrain/structure reflections.

Escalate output only after the prior level satisfies safety gates. Extreme-output runs should be remote/unmanned inside the exposure zone.

## Distance and scale matrix
Suggested progression:
- ~10 m;
- 25–50 m;
- 100 m;
- longer range only if effects remain measurable and the site supports safe testing.

Increase fire size only after characterizing the acoustic field and suppression response at the previous scale.

**Do not infer the scaling law from stadium/auditorium perception. Measure it at the fire.**

## Closed-loop targeting
Thermal/optical sensing identifies flame-front geometry, hot spots, ember ejection zones, and spot ignitions. DSP and suppressant delivery may then retarget within the validated envelope.

Compare fixed settings with adaptive targeting under wind-perturbed conditions.

## Safety gates
No high-output test without:
- burn/incident commander authority;
- full conventional suppression backup;
- exclusion zones based on measured acoustics, not assumed rear nulls;
- hearing/infrasound exposure controls;
- remote operation for extreme-SPL conditions;
- structural/vibration monitoring;
- wildlife/ecological review;
- airspace coordination and no interference with aviation;
- emergency stop under fire-command control;
- predefined weather/wind abort thresholds;
- emergency/medical planning.

Rear rejection is an engineering feature, not a guarantee of silence or safety.

## Success criteria
A positive result requires a reproducible, instrumented improvement versus the appropriate control, such as:
- higher extinction probability;
- shorter extinction time;
- lower flame height/thermal output;
- slower spread;
- reduced ember generation;
- improved suppressant penetration;
- reduced reignition.

A dramatic visual response without corroborating measurements is insufficient.

## Failure / downgrade criteria
Downgrade or reject the scaling hypothesis if target-side forcing cannot be maintained with increasing distance, acoustics show no reproducible effect, acoustics worsen spread/embers, rear exposure cannot be controlled adequately, harms exceed plausible benefit, or acoustic+mist provides no advantage over conventional suppression.

**Null results are Receipts.**

## Escalation ladder
1. Instrumented established burn tray / vegetation bed.
2. Larger vegetation front under controlled airflow.
3. Outdoor controlled plot with realistic variability.
4. Established-fire augmentation during a professionally managed experimental/prescribed burn.
5. Operational prototype evaluation only after independent replication and formal safety review.

No direct jump from laboratory success to uncontrolled wildfire deployment.

## Falsifiable hypotheses
**H1:** Concert-scale, large-aperture low-frequency forcing measurably changes established vegetation-fire behavior.

**H2:** Acoustic + mist/retardant outperforms mist/retardant alone for at least one defined established-fire class.

**H3:** Directional/cardioid design materially improves target-side forcing / rear-zone exposure ratio.

**H4:** Suppression benefit remains measurable as distance and fire scale increase through at least one outdoor controlled-fire regime.

**H5:** Closed-loop thermal/optical retargeting improves performance relative to fixed targeting under variable flame-front geometry and wind.

## Design philosophy
**Do not assume large-fire scaling works. Do not assume it fails. Treat scaling as the experiment.**

**Maximum extinction probability first. Scaling law second. Efficiency third.**
