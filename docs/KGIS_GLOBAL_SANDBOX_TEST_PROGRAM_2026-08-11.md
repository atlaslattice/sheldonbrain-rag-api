# KGIS Global Sandbox Test Program

**Date:** 2026-08-11

## Purpose

Create a two-track research program that gives high-risk, high-upside hypotheses global computational breadth while keeping physical intervention staged, reversible, and evidence-gated.

The program intentionally separates:

1. **Global simulation for breadth** — digital twin / scenario exploration across climate, ecology, fire resilience, infrastructure, and proposed KAPPA assumptions.
2. **Local experiments for truth** — contained, replicated, instrumented tests that determine whether the simulated assumptions survive contact with reality.

No global physical ecological intervention is authorized by simulation alone.

---

## Track A — Ecological Architecture Hypothesis

### Core hypothesis

An engineered sanctuary ecosystem that minimizes or excludes insects, parasites, and predators may outperform conventional reference ecosystems on defined ecological-service metrics.

This is a hypothesis, not an established result.

### Simulation worlds

- **ECO-A / Reference:** Current best-supported ecology, including insects, parasites, predators, decomposers, pollinators, and trophic interactions.
- **ECO-B / Reduced guilds:** Selected insect, parasite, or predator guilds reduced by parameterized percentages.
- **ECO-C / Functional replacement:** Reduced guilds plus engineered replacements for lost functions: robotic pollination, managed decomposition, nutrient cycling, disease control, seed dispersal, and population regulation.
- **ECO-D / Founder hypothesis:** Near-total exclusion of insects, parasites, and predators with all required ecosystem services explicitly supplied or redesigned.

### Required metrics

- Net primary productivity and useful biomass yield
- Plant reproductive success
- Pollination success and fruit/seed set
- Soil organic matter and nutrient cycling
- Soil microbiome stability and diversity
- Decomposition rate and litter accumulation
- Water retention / infiltration / evapotranspiration
- Carbon retention and fire-fuel accumulation
- Disease burden and plant mortality
- Invasive-species pressure
- Biodiversity and functional redundancy
- Food-web stability
- Recovery after drought, flood, heat, and fire
- Multi-year stability and tipping-point behavior

### Test rule

The no-insect/no-predator architecture does not "win" by producing more biomass alone. It must meet or exceed the reference system across a predeclared multi-metric scorecard and remain stable across multiple simulated years and disturbances.

### Physical progression

**Stage E0 — Digital twin only**

Parameter sweep across climates and ecosystem types.

**Stage E1 — Laboratory microcosms**

Contained soil/plant communities with controlled guild presence/absence.

**Stage E2 — Mesocosms**

Greenhouse or fenced outdoor plots with replication and no ecological escape pathway.

**Stage E3 — Bounded field trials**

Small, reversible, monitored plots with matched controls and local ecological oversight.

**Stage E4 — Regional replication**

Only after independently reproduced benefits and explicit ecological-safety review.

**No continental or global physical removal of functional guilds occurs before these gates are passed.**

## Track B — KAPPA / Electron-Exclusion Hypothesis

### Core measurable claim

Avoid metaphysical language in the engineering layer. Test the measurable proposition:

> Electron-bound states may suppress or alter functions that become available, enhanced, or qualitatively different in electron-excluded states.

For deuterium, the candidate state is the bare deuteron / electron-excluded state, denoted in project terminology as **D^kappa**.

### Simulation worlds

- **KAPPA-A / Standard physics:** Established atomic, plasma, nuclear, thermal, and electromagnetic models.
- **KAPPA-B / Electron-exclusion model:** Same baseline plus user-specified hypotheses for electron-excluded state behavior.
- **KAPPA-C / Sensitivity envelope:** Sweep uncertain parameters across conservative, neutral, and aggressive assumptions.

### Model outputs

- Energy balance and net energy gain/loss
- Charge confinement requirements
- Space-charge behavior
- Electron recombination rates
- Thermal load
- Magnetic/electric field requirements
- Material stresses
- Water/energy production claims, if modeled
- Failure modes and runaway conditions
- Estimated observables that distinguish KAPPA predictions from standard physics

### Falsification requirement

Every KAPPA simulation must emit at least one measurable discriminator:

- expected spectrum / signal
- energy delta
- charge-state lifetime
- field response
- thermal signature
- particle distribution
- reaction products, if any

If no measurable discriminator exists, the scenario is not test-ready.

### Physical progression

**Stage K0 — Simulation and literature baseline**

Reproduce standard predictions before adding new assumptions.

**Stage K1 — Charge-state experiment**

Create and verify bare deuterons / electron-excluded states using established instrumentation.

**Stage K2 — Confinement / modulation test**

Test proposed vibroacoustic, optical, magnetic, and electrostatic control mechanisms one at a time before combinations.

**Stage K3 — Combined KAPPA testbed**

Run the minimum integrated system required to test a predeclared anomaly prediction.

**Stage K4 — Replication**

Independent apparatus, operators, and analysis.

**Stage K5 — Engineering scale-up**

Only after reproducible nonstandard effect and closed energy accounting.

## Coupled Global Sandbox

The global sandbox may model both tracks together while keeping epistemic layers explicit.

Example scenario matrix:

| Scenario | Ecology | Power/Water | Fire Defense | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | Conventional ecology | Conventional | Conventional | Reference |
| GGW-1 | Regenerative ecology | Conventional | Detection + mist + mesh | Supported engineering |
| GGW-2 | Regenerative ecology | Conventional | Acoustic + mist experimental | Candidate |
| GGW-3 | Founder ecology hypothesis | Conventional | Full fire stack | Hypothesis |
| GGW-4 | Regenerative ecology | KAPPA assumptions | Full fire stack | Separately gated |
| GGW-5 | Founder ecology hypothesis | KAPPA assumptions | Full fire stack | High-speculation sandbox only |

This prevents a positive result in one speculative layer from being mistaken for validation of another.

## Epistemic Labels

Every model component and output carries one label:

- **ESTABLISHED** — reproduced conventional science / engineering
- **SUPPORTED** — multiple credible studies, limited scaling uncertainty
- **CANDIDATE** — plausible engineering extrapolation requiring validation
- **HYPOTHESIS** — unvalidated proposed mechanism
- **SPECULATIVE** — depends on multiple unvalidated assumptions
- **FALSIFIED / REJECTED** — failed predeclared test

No output may silently cross these boundaries.

---

## Required Controls

Every experiment or simulation includes matched controls wherever possible.

### Ecology controls

- intact ecosystem reference
- disturbance-only control
- guild-removal without replacement
- guild-removal with engineered functional replacement

### KAPPA controls

- neutral deuterium
- conventional deuterium plasma / ion baseline where applicable
- bare deuteron control under established confinement
- sham acoustic / optical / field modulation

---

## Global Sandbox Outputs

The platform should produce:

1. **World maps** of predicted performance, risk, and uncertainty.
2. **Sensitivity maps** showing which assumptions dominate outcomes.
3. **Failure maps** showing where the architecture breaks.
4. **Experiment priority queue** ranked by expected information gain per dollar and per unit risk.
5. **Receipts ledger** linking every model parameter to source, observation, or explicit founder hypothesis.
6. **Counterfactual comparison** against conventional reforestation, wildfire resilience, energy, and ecological-management strategies.

---

## Primary Invariant

> **Global simulation for breadth. Local experiments for truth.**

A global sandbox is encouraged to explore extreme hypotheses. The real biosphere is not used as the first uncontrolled experiment.

## Collaboration Note

This specification intentionally allows high-speculation ideas to be modeled without treating them as established fact. That is the point of the sandbox: hypotheses are allowed to be ambitious; physical deployment earns permission through measurement.
